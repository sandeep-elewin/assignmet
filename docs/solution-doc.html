1. Framing and assumptions

The brief is precise about what a promo is, so I made its three tests the gate, not a score: a clip ships only if a hook lands inside the first three seconds, the cut leaves an unresolved moment, and a newcomer can follow it. Ranking is a separate, later question. Mixing the two is how a tool ends up confidently posting a bad clip.

  • Input is the screenplay pack only. No video or audio, so every judgement is made on text and timecodes. I did not listen to any cut.
  • A promo may be one continuous stretch or two segments joined inside the same episode. I did not join across episodes: the 50 files share characters but not a continuous timeline I can trust, and a cross-episode jump is a much harder standalone-sense problem.
  • The output is what an editor needs to make the cut, not a rendered file: episode, in/out per segment, the hook line, the cliff line, and a one-line rationale. Subtitle and music choices are out of scope.
  • The show's own emotion parentheticals are free supervision. Every line is tagged (Devastated), (Cold, Menacing); that turned out to be the single strongest hook feature, so the lexicon is built around it.

2. How it works

Parse every camera, action, sound and dialogue block into absolute-time beats; propose candidates; score them; rank. Boundaries are arithmetic, so rules do them exactly: 12 anchors per episode × 3 out-points, plus the episode's own ending, = 459 candidates (9.2/episode), all snapped to beat boundaries.

FeatureHow it is measuredWeight
Hookbest beat in the first 3s: emotion intensity, short high-voltage line, reveal / slap / hard cut0.30
Cliffthe last spoken line: question, trailing ellipsis, threat, reveal, plus a cut-to-black tag0.30
Standaloneseries-wide character debt: names the viewer has not met (e.g. "the Dowager"), alias-merged across all 50 files0.22
Arcemotional peak position and lift from opening to peak0.12
Pacingspeech-to-runtime ratio and cuts per 10s0.06

Score = 100 × weighted sum × duration fit (full credit 28–45s, small decay outside). A clip must also clear 0.45 hook / 0.45 cliff / 0.50 standalone. Episodes that clear nothing are reported as "no postable promo" with the reason, not forced into the list. Two editorial passes run before the gate: tail tightening (cut at the last strong line if everything after it deflates) and stitching (join a strong opener to a strong closer, but only if the montage ends on a spoken line, adds ≥0.15 to hook+cliff, and loses ≤1.0 point elsewhere). Final ranking takes one pick per episode first, then a second pass, rejecting overlaps above 40%.

3. Code, model, human — what did which job

CodeModelHuman (me)Why
Parsing, timecodes, boundary snapping, length limits, gate arithmetic, dedupe, ranking, JSON/HTML. All deterministic; same pack → same list. An optional judge over the top 2 clips per episode: hook / cliff / standalone / spoiler risk / postable, plus a reason and one fix. Blended 55% model, 45% rules. Never trusted on timestamps. Chose the problem and the three-test framing; set the weights by looking at the score distribution; read all 15 top transcripts; overrode the tool's #1 pick; decided when to stop tuning. Boundaries are arithmetic and should not cost a token. "Would a stranger understand this?" is judgement and should not be a regex. Tuning is neither.

The model is swappable by environment variable only — OpenAI-compatible (PROMO_LLM_BASE_URL, so Groq, OpenRouter, vLLM, Ollama all work) or Anthropic. No key ⇒ the heuristic pass runs and says so. Verdicts are cached by content hash.

Three defects the audit found, all fixed and now covered by tests:

  1. Reported cliff ≠ actual ending. The scorer took the highest-scoring line in the last 4s. On ep32 that was "A disgraced woman?", while the clip ran on to "…You cannot destroy me." — a resolved, triumphant ending. The card was describing a cut that was never made. The cliff is now the last spoken beat. Ep32 and ep3 dropped out (correctly); ep18's better narrative cut surfaced at #1.
  2. Clips opened mid-sentence. The screenplays split lines across blocks ("…no one will remember…"). An ep29 pick started on the back half. The in-point now walks back to the sentence's first beat (max 10s).
  3. Stitching won for the wrong reason. A flat score margin let ep1's montage win by jumping 35s and ending on "Cut to black", while ep45's genuinely good montage lost. Stitching now requires both bookends to improve, a spoken final line, and a bounded loss elsewhere.

Known bad cases I did not paper over. Three picks open on a fragment that answers an unheard question (#11 "No.", #12 "It is medicine!", #14 "I didn't!") — a reply-aware hook penalty would fix it but costs recall on punchy one-liners, so it is a human trim. Two picks have a procedural middle (#8 measuring a pulse, #9 reciting a journal) yet score pacing 1.00, because pacing only sees speech ratio and cut rate, not new information. ep50's best hook — "Absolution is free. Discretion has a price." — was refused at 0.42 because the line is delivered flat and has no emotion tag: the tags are doing a lot of the work. Of the 11 refusals, I judge 3 (ep21, ep39, ep49) to be false negatives and 6 clearly right; the rest fail on a visual hook the text cannot see.

5. Scale, cost, and the next hour of work

At volume. Heuristic pass: ~0.12s per episode on one core, embarrassingly parallel. Model pass: 2 clips per episode, ~1,150 input / ~120 output tokens per call — about $0.02 per 50-episode season on gpt-4o-mini, $0.14 on Haiku, $0.50 on Sonnet at list prices; caching makes re-runs free. 500 episodes is ~1 minute of rules and 20 cents of models.

Human review is the real cost. Fifteen full transcripts took ~35 minutes. At 500 episodes, review only the top 40 and a random 10 for precision; everything else is a backlog. The tool is built to make that queue small and honest.

If I had a second day:

  • A "reply-aware" hook penalty for one-word answers, and a cliff_kind: visual flag so wordless endings are phrased as an image, not blocking.
  • An information-delta feature for pacing (new facts per 10s), so procedural scenes stop scoring 1.00.
  • A series-trailer mode: 3–5 moments across episodes. The stitcher already builds the graph; the missing piece is a cross-episode standalone check.
  • Test the 15 cuts against retention on a real account — no text heuristic replaces that signal.
